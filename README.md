The hospitality industry operates in an environment of persistent demand uncertainty, and hotel booking cancellations represent one of its most consequential operational challenges. As online travel platforms have normalized flexible booking policies that allow guests to cancel with little or no financial penalty, the rate of cancellations across the industry has risen steadily. For hotel revenue managers, this creates a recurring problem: rooms that appear occupied on paper may ultimately go empty, while last-minute vacancies are difficult to refill at competitive rates. 

This project addresses the problem as a supervised binary classification task. The target variable is whether a given booking will be canceled (is_canceled = 1) or not (is_canceled = 0). By training machine learning models on historical booking data, we aim to identify the characteristics that most reliably distinguish canceling guests from non-canceling guests, and to produce predictions accurate enough to support proactive revenue management decisions at the property level. 

The project pursues three complementary research questions: (1) Which booking characteristics significantly affect the probability of cancellation? (2) Which customer segments carry the highest cancellation risk? (3) Can cancellations be predicted with sufficient accuracy for operational deployment? These questions are addressed using logistic regression, K-Means clustering, and XGBoost respectively, together covering the spectrum from interpretability to predictive performance.
